# Project Description for Amazon

## Introduction

Amazon is the most widely used online store around the world. After its success in e-commerce, the company has branched out into cloud computing, digital streaming, artificial intelligence, and more. But most people still know Amazon for its online store, connecting customers to vendors across the globe and giving them better deals and a bigger variety of items than a brick-and-mortar store ever could.

The scenario and problems in this chapter revolve around Amazon's online store and the deals it offers its customers.

## Statement

Imagine you're a developer on Amazon's online store team. Your team is working on several features related to product recommendations, promoting special deals, and upselling items. Alongside these customer-facing features, you're also optimizing delivery fleet management and integrating data from other e-commerce companies Amazon has acquired.

## Features

Here's the list of features the team wants built:

1. **Free delivery suggestions** — if a customer's total bill is close to the free-shipping threshold, recommend items to add to the cart so they qualify.
2. **Special offer bundles** — some customers have won a coupon worth $200 to buy up to three items; suggest a bundle of three items that fits their coupon.
3. **Upselling products** — upsell a random product from a set of related products, recommending it to users at checkout.
4. **Copy product data** — create a deep copy of a list of products from a recently acquired company's website so it can be imported into Amazon.
5. **Order processing milestones** — mine daily stats to populate an order-processing dashboard.
6. **Products frequently viewed together** — recommend items that are frequently viewed alongside the one a customer is looking at.
7. **Optimize delivery cost** — help the logistics division make the best use of contractor delivery trucks.
8. **Merge recommendations** — merge product recommendations with data from an acquired company.
9. **Products in price range** — let a user filter the product list by a minimum and maximum price.
10. **Shopping cart total cost** — revamp the shopping cart so it calculates the total bill, including any discounts.
11. **Ad serving** — let a client control how many ads are displayed on their screen.
12. **Warehouse and drop points** — help a warehouse robot plan its trip from open space regions to the nearest drop points.
13. **Time-based item price store** — track item prices at different timestamps and retrieve an item's price as of any given timestamp.
14. **Find similar products** — find users with similar tastes by returning the products two or more users have both purchased.

Take a moment before reading on: how would *you* implement each of these? By the end of this chapter you'll see the same handful of techniques — two-pointers, hashing, heaps, graphs, binary search, and dynamic programming — show up again and again, both here and in classic interview questions.
